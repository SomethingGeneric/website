# Crystal Linux is dead
## And I'm not sure if it's successor will happen

I've really enjoyed working on Crystal. It meant a lot to me to have connections with awesome people all over the world, and it really did help me through the monotony of COVID, nevermind the technical skills and Linux-foo I gained along the way.

Additionally, the community we created in Discord, the forms, and beyond (RIP mailing lists) was and still is awesome, even though it's petered out in a big way.

And, that's ok, because everyone involved in building Crystal have moved on. Life has changed, COVID is (mostly??) over, and we all have new jobs, school, life evenets, etc.

Nevermind the fact that we built it, it works, and it's going to *keep* working unless someone finds some hidden vulnerability or issue in the tools that we don't know about.

That said, as per the announcement by Eryn in the Discord, what we all once knew as Crystal Linux is no more.

For folks that still use it, you're on your own.

This post serves as an announcement that I'm happy to help support people using Crystal transition into using Arch, and troubleshoot anything that may happen along the way. (I'm sure any other former Crystal folks using Arch would be happy to do the same, but I'm not sure how many of them do...?)

In addition, I'd like to mention two things:

## Project Shards

Amy and Eryn (and others?) are working on a "new" Crystal, called Project Shards, which you can read about in Eryn's announcement in the (newly rebranded) Discord.

I wish them the best of luck, but personally I've moved on to just using base Arch, with some custom tooling here and there.

Which would bring me to my next announcement:

## Krypton Linux

In the future:tm: I'm going to be working on Krypton Linux, a non-systemd Arch fork, with the goal of proving that a modern, easy to use, desktop Linux setup is still possible without systemd and the baggage that comes along with it.

Before you ask, no, I don't hate systemd. I'm very thankful for the fact that it put an end to the init-wars of the early 2000's, and that we didn't get stuck with Upstart (f*** off, Canonical.).

However, I dislike how it's grown to become the "standard" for Linux, in both servers and workstations. I want to show that while it's a nice-to-have, it's not necesary.

And more importantly, that it's possible to live your life, use your PC with Linux, and not care what a mailing list of people who (mostly) work for IBM think about how your Linux system should work. * (See links below)

Given that I'm in my last year of college, doing my senior project, and working three jobs(!!), I can't promise a timeline or anything.

What little I've been able to do thus far can be found at: https://gitlab.goober.cloud/krypton . 

If you're a former Crystal user, do note that I won't be supporting Onyx/GNOME (too far down the systemd/IBM rabbit-hole), but I **will** be aiming to support/continue Amethyst, which may take some time since I still haven't actually learned rust.

## TL;DR

* Crystal is dead
* Long live Project Shards and Krypton
* I am working too much
* Let me know if you need help with your "legacy" Crystal installs.

Thanks for reading.
